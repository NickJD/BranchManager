Evidence quality

Evidence quality is the assessment's own category; it is not a probability of correct identification.

How to read this:

“Pass with warnings” means the marker was retained with qualifications. “Fail QC” means it did not meet the normal marker quality requirements. Any manual review or override should be examined in the underlying evidence before a sequencing decision is made.

Important interpretation:

A high sequence identity does not automatically guarantee an unambiguous taxonomic assignment. Query coverage describes how much of the submitted sequence aligns; reference coverage describes how much of the reference sequence is covered. Conflicting near-best reference assignments can result in a contested classification even when the alignment is long and highly similar.

Novelty explorer

Compare each marker against the cultured baselines, wider partner collection and external reference set. These are sequence-similarity comparisons, not species novelty claims.
How to interpret these comparisons:

Nearest identity is the highest sequence identity found in that comparison set. The ≥99%, ≥97% and ≥95% counts describe the number of reported neighbours meeting those operational similarity cut-offs. They are not formal species boundaries. A low identity to a cultured baseline can coexist with a high identity to an external reference; that means the isolate may be poorly represented in the cultured collection while having a close external reference match.
